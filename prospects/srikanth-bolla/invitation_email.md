## MIT / "MIT Sloan" claim — discrepancy flagged, not resolved by guessing

The row's `careerBackground` field says "First international blind student admitted to MIT Sloan."
Srikanth's own LinkedIn About section says "MIT's first international blind student" (no "Sloan"
qualifier), and his LinkedIn Experience section lists only one MIT-affiliated role: **"Undergraduate
Research Assistant," The Sinha Lab for Developmental Research, MIT, Feb 2009 – May 2013** — an
undergraduate research-assistant position, not an MIT Sloan graduate-program enrollment.

Independent sources corroborate the underlying facts but sharpen where the ambiguity actually sits:
BusinessToday (2024-04-11) and several other outlets state he earned an **SB (bachelor's) in
Management Science** from MIT, and that he was admitted in **2009** — a timeframe that lines up
exactly with the LinkedIn Sinha Lab dates (2009–2013). MIT's Management Science major is
administratively housed in the **Sloan School of Management**, so "MIT Sloan" is not simply wrong —
but "Sloan" popularly evokes MIT Sloan's separate **graduate** programs (the MBA, or the mid-career
"Sloan Fellows" program, which by design doesn't admit students straight out of school). Srikanth was
an undergraduate, not a Sloan Fellow or MBA student. **This file treats "admitted to MIT" as the
safely corroborated core fact and deliberately drops "Sloan" from the email and LinkedIn message** to
avoid implying the graduate program — but does not independently resolve which framing ("MIT" vs.
"MIT Sloan") Srikanth himself would prefer stated in print; worth a direct question to him if the
distinction matters for the final programme materials.
